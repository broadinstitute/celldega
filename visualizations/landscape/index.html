    Landscape

    Landscape shows where cells are and what they express. In spatial data, it draws tissue images, cells, transcripts, and neighborhoods together, so you can ask where a cell type sits in the tissue, which cells express a gene, and which cells neighbor each other. The same cells can also be shown in a low dimensional embedding, such as a UMAP.

    Spatial Landscapes read DegaFiles, loading the image, cell, and transcript tiles needed for the current view. This page covers the spatial view. UMAP and XY covers embeddings and single-cell data, and Neighborhoods covers drawing and using tissue regions and gates.

    Reading a Landscape

    Close up, a Landscape layers the microscopy image, each cell's segmentation boundary and centroid, and every detected transcript. Cells are colored by cluster, and transcripts by gene. The bar graphs in the control panel summarize what is in view: the CELL bars count cells per cluster, and the TRX bars count transcripts per gene.

    A glomerulus in a whole-transcriptome kidney section. Podocytes (cluster 16) lead the CELL bars and the podocyte gene PODXL leads the TRX bars, because they are what is in view.

    Control What it does
    1 View and image SPATIAL, UMAP, and XY switch views; IMG shows or hides the image; each channel button toggles a channel, and its slider sets the channel's brightness.
    2 CELL Shows or hides cells; the slider sets their size. The bars rank clusters by the number of cells in view.
    3 TRX Transcripts and gene expression. Its button and slider depend on the zoom; see Genes and transcripts. The bars rank genes.
    4 Gene search Color cells by one or more genes. The box below describes the selected gene.
    5 Celldega logo Opens this page.
    6 The view Scroll to zoom, drag to pan, hover for details, and click to select.

    The control panel is shared with Yearbook, CellCloud, and NeighborhoodCloud; see the control panel.

    Zooming and panning

    Scroll to zoom and drag to pan. A Landscape uses semantic zoom: what it draws, and what its controls do, change with the zoom level.

    • Zoomed out, each cell is a dot colored by cluster or gene expression, over the whole tissue.
    • Closer in, the image appears at higher resolution, and TRX can load the transcripts and cell boundaries in view on request.
    • Close up, transcripts and cell boundaries load automatically for the tiles in view.

    Because a Landscape only loads the tiles in view, it stays responsive even for datasets with over a billion transcripts. See Performance.

    Scrolling into a glomerulus: cell boundaries and transcripts load as the view narrows, and the TRX button becomes active.

    Double-clicking does not zoom. The zoom stops slightly beyond the whole tissue and at about 10 µm across the view; min_zoom and max_zoom change these limits.

    Images

    IMG shows or hides the image. Each channel, such as DAPI or a membrane stain, has a button to show or hide it and a slider for its brightness. When an aligned H&E image has been added to the DegaFiles, an FLR / H&E selector next to IMG switches between the fluorescence channels and H&E; see Add aligned H&E to DegaFiles.

    Hiding the cells, turning off the boundary and RNA channels, adjusting DAPI brightness, and restoring them.

    When you select a cluster or gene while zoomed out, the image is hidden so that the selected cells stand out. It returns when you zoom in close or clear the selection.

    Cells and clusters

    CELL shows or hides the cells, and its slider sets their size. Click a cluster's bar to show only that cluster; click it again to show all clusters. Shift-click more bars to add clusters to the selection. Hover a bar for its cell count. You can also click a cell in the view to select its cluster.

    Selecting podocytes (cluster 16) outlines every glomerulus in the section; shift-clicking adds glomerular endothelial cells (cluster 20).

    Selecting a cluster clears any gene selection.

    Genes and transcripts

    Click a gene's bar, or type its name in the gene search box, to color cells by its expression. Type several names separated by commas, or shift-click several bars, to color cells by their combined (summed) expression; up to the 20 highest-expressed genes are combined. Clear the search box, or type cluster, to return to cluster colors.

    Searching for the podocyte marker NPHS2 and the endothelial marker EMCN lights up every glomerulus across the section; zooming into one shows the two genes' individual transcripts.

    The TRX controls change with the zoom

    The TRX button and slider are an example of semantic zoom:

    Zoom TRX button TRX slider
    Close up Blue or gray: shows or hides transcripts, which load automatically Transcript size
    Closer in Gray: click to load the transcripts and boundaries in view Cell expression opacity, once a gene is selected
    Zoomed out Faded: transcripts are not available Cell expression opacity, once a gene is selected

    Zoomed out, a selected gene colors cells, and the slider sets how strongly; the gene box shows the expression that reaches full color, such as Full opacity at ≥ 145. Close up, the same selection shows only those genes' transcripts, each colored by gene, and the slider sets their size.

    Zoomed out: cells colored by the combined expression of NPHS2 and EMCN. Every glomerulus stands out; the fainter signal elsewhere fits EMCN in endothelial cells outside the glomeruli.

    Close up, with cells hidden: NPHS2 transcripts (teal) along the podocytes and EMCN transcripts (yellow) in the capillaries, two orthogonal markers in one glomerulus.

    The gene box describes the selected genes using UniProt (human gene symbols). Clicking a transcript selects its gene.

    Sequencing-based spatial data

    Sequencing-based technologies such as Visium HD measure expression in a grid of small bins rather than as individual transcripts. To show this as transcripts, preprocessing creates one point per count in each bin and adds a small random offset, or jitter, so that a bin's points spread out instead of drawing on top of each other. Zoomed in, each bin then shows the mix of genes it detected, in their gene colors, and the same gene controls work as for imaging data. The positions within a bin are a visual aid, not measured locations.

    Visium HD colorectal cancer: zooming from the whole section into normal crypts reveals jittered transcripts, and the TRX bars are led by plasma-cell genes (IGKC, PIGR, JCHAIN). Selecting IGKC and hiding the H&E shows its transcripts in the plasma cells around the crypts; zooming out shows IGKC across the section.

    Celldega's preprocessing writes these points with pre.write_pseudotranscripts_from_sbg, whose jitter argument sets the range of the offset; see the pre API. The Visium HD colorectal cancer tutorial explores this dataset with a linked Clustergram and Enrich.

    Spaces and embeddings

    The same cells can be shown in more than one space: their positions in the tissue, and a low dimensional embedding, such as a UMAP computed from their expression. Pass an AnnData with obsm["X_umap"], for example from Scanpy, along with the DegaFiles, and UMAP and SPATIAL move the cells between the two spaces. The move is animated so that you can follow the cells. Select a cluster in one space, then switch to the other to see where those cells are.

    Xenium pancreas: zoomed out on the tissue, the cells move into their Scanpy UMAP. Selecting the islet cluster (9) highlights it in the UMAP, and switching back to SPATIAL shows the islets in the tissue.

    In both spaces, the bar graphs describe the cells in view, so zooming into a region is a way to query its neighborhood: a tissue region in the spatial view, or a group of similar cells in the UMAP.

    The Scanpy–Squidpy Xenium pancreas tutorial computes this UMAP with Scanpy and passes it to the Landscape. The AnnData's cell IDs must match the DegaFiles:

    adata.obs_names = adata.obs["cell_id"].astype(str)  # Xenium cell IDs
landscape = dega.viz.Landscape(base_url=base_url, adata=adata, cluster_attr="leiden")

    See UMAP and XY for more about embeddings.

    Hovering for details

    Hover over a cell for its ID and cluster, and its expression when cells are colored by a gene. Hover over a transcript for its gene, with the protein name and description from UniProt.

    Hovering a transcript shows its gene, here NES (Nestin), with a description.

    Single-cell Landscapes

    For single-cell RNA-seq, a Landscape needs no DegaFiles. Save your Scanpy AnnData as an AnnData Zarr store with a CSC copy of the expression matrix, and the browser reads the cells, their UMAP, and one gene at a time directly from the store. Add a Clustergram of cell-type marker genes for a linked Landscape–Clustergram. Spatial data needs one more step, preparing DegaFiles for the images, transcripts, and boundaries, but single-cell data goes straight from an AnnData:

    import scanpy as sc
import celldega as dega

# Cells: a CSC copy lets the browser read one gene at a time.
adata.layers["counts_csc"] = adata.layers["counts"].tocsc()
adata.write_zarr("cells.zarr")

# Cell-type marker genes as a dot plot, clustered on both axes.
sc.tl.rank_genes_groups(adata, groupby="cell_type")
markers = dega.clust.Matrix(adata, groupby="cell_type")
markers.norm("row", by="zscore")
markers.cluster(view="rank_genes_groups", levels=[1, 3, 5, 10])

landscape = dega.viz.Landscape(
    zarr_url="cells.zarr", zarr_layer="counts_csc", cluster_attr="cell_type"
)
cgm = dega.viz.Clustergram(matrix=markers, rank_dim=3)
dega.viz.spatial_clustergram(landscape, cgm)

    In a single-cell Landscape, the bars describe the cells in view. As you pan and zoom, the CELL bars count the cells of each type in view, which gives the local cell-type proportions. With cell-type signatures, the gene bars estimate the mean expression of the cells in view from those proportions, and TRX turns purple. See viewport estimates.

    Zooming in therefore queries the local neighborhood you are looking at, in data space here just as in tissue space for spatial data: which cell types are in this part of the UMAP, and which genes characterize them. Click a gene from those bars, then zoom out to see how localized its expression is.

    Tabula Sapiens: zooming into the swirl of spermatids at the top of the UMAP, the CELL bars narrow to spermatids and spermatocytes, and TRX turns purple. Selecting the top estimated gene, RGMB-AS1, and zooming out shows its expression concentrated in the swirl.

    Explore the 1.1-million-cell atlas with its linked marker Clustergram in the Gallery, or follow the Tabula Sapiens notebook to build the Landscape, Clustergram, and Enrich from AnnData Zarr stores. The Chromium PBMC tutorial walks through the same steps from a Scanpy analysis.

    Multiple datasets

    Pass a list of DegaFiles URLs to switch between datasets in one Landscape. A dropdown after IMG lists them, and switching keeps the selected clusters, genes, and view where possible:

    landscape = dega.viz.Landscape(
    base_url=["https://host/sample_a", "https://host/sample_b"],
    dataset_names=["Sample A", "Sample B"],
)

    Each item can also be a dictionary with url, label, and short_label.

    Linking to other visualizations

    Linked to a Clustergram with dega.viz.spatial_clustergram, a Landscape responds to Clustergram clicks: a gene colors cells by its expression, a cluster selects its cells, and a gene group colors cells by the genes' combined expression. See the Clustergram's linking table. Selections made in the Landscape do not change the Clustergram.

    Widget: Python-backed interactions

    As a notebook widget, a Landscape also shares state with Python:

    • Selected cells: landscape.selected_cells holds the cells of the selected clusters, a drawn neighborhood, or cells highlighted from a linked Clustergram. landscape.get_selected_cells(adata) returns them as an AnnData.
    • View: landscape.landscape_state is "spatial", "umap", or "xy".
    • Neighborhoods: drawn regions are available as landscape.nbhd; see Neighborhoods.
    • Updates from Python: landscape.update_cell_clusters(...) recolors cells with new cluster labels, and landscape.highlight_cells(...) highlights cells.

    The zoom, pan, slider positions, and selected genes are not sent back to Python.

    Use it from Python

    import celldega as dega

landscape = dega.viz.Landscape(
    base_url="https://your-landscape-files-url",
    adata=adata,
    ini_zoom=-5,
)
landscape

    The optional adata supplies cell attributes, cluster colors, and UMAP coordinates; its obs_names must match the DegaFiles cell IDs. The segmentation shown is chosen when the widget is created, with segmentation=.

    To add an aligned H&E background to prepared DegaFiles, use dega.pre.add_aligned_he("data/DegaFiles", "sample_he.ome.tif", alignment="sample_he_imagealignment.csv"), then reload the Landscape. See Add aligned H&E to DegaFiles.

    For constructor arguments and methods, see the Viz API. For web pages without Python, see the JavaScript API.
